# The question library

Fourteen moves. Each one opens a specific kind of door; none of them is a step in a sequence. Read
this page when the last answer left the conversation stuck — pick the move whose door is shut, not
the next one in the list.

Every example below is in the user's language, at the abstraction level the skill requires: a
question a person can answer from his own experience in one breath.

## Materials — what is actually there

### 1. Definition

- **Opens:** whether two people are using one word for two things.
- **Shape:** 「你說 <word> 的時候，指什麼？給我一個具體的例子。」
- **Failure:** accepting an abstract restatement as a definition. A definition that cannot produce
  an example has not been given yet.

### 2. Smallest case

- **Opens:** the load-bearing core, stripped of scale, budget and staging.
- **Shape:** 「這件事最小的一個版本長什麼樣？小到什麼程度仍然算它？」
- **Failure:** the user describes the smallest *deliverable* instead of the smallest *case*.

### 3. Origin

- **Opens:** whether a belief rests on an observation or on a sentence someone else said.
- **Shape:** 「這個看法是從哪一次經驗來的？」
- **Failure:** asking 「為什麼」 instead. Why invites a story built after the fact; where-from asks
  for a memory.

## Judgements — what counts as what

### 4. Criterion

- **Opens:** the rule the user is actually applying, usually unconsciously.
- **Shape:** 「你怎麼知道這一次屬於 <category>？上一次你憑什麼判斷它不是？」
- **Failure:** the user answers with examples. Ask again for the rule the examples share.

### 5. Boundary

- **Opens:** where the category stops, which is where the interesting disagreement lives.
- **Shape:** 「什麼情況不算？差在哪一點上？」
- **Failure:** accepting "it depends" without the axis it depends on.

### 6. Counterexample

- **Opens:** the description's precision, by putting a case in front of it that nearly fits.
- **Shape:** 「有沒有明明符合你剛才的描述，但你覺得不是的情況？」
- **Failure:** supplying the counterexample from the agent's own imagination. The user's own
  near-miss is the only one that tests his own description.

### 7. Abstraction

- **Opens:** the invariant under the domain words.
- **Shape:** 「把剛才那句裡的專業名詞全部拿掉，剩下的是什麼？」
- **Failure:** swapping one jargon word for another jargon word.

## Purpose — what it is for

### 8. Purpose

- **Opens:** whether the stated goal is a goal or a method wearing a goal's clothes.
- **Shape:** 「這件事成了之後，什麼會變得不一樣？誰會察覺到這個不一樣？」
- **Failure:** the answer restates the method. Ask who notices, and what they notice.

### 9. Failure

- **Opens:** the success criteria, from the other side, where vagueness cannot hide.
- **Shape:** 「什麼情況你會說這次失敗了？」
- **Failure:** a failure condition that is merely the absence of the success condition. Push for the
  observable event.

### 10. Cost

- **Opens:** the price that is being paid without being counted.
- **Shape:** 「維持它需要付出什麼？誰在付？」
- **Failure:** stopping at money. Time, attention, optionality and the trust of a specific person are
  all currencies.

## Structure — how it holds together

### 11. Consequence

- **Opens:** whether the claim is load-bearing or decorative.
- **Shape:** 「如果它是真的，會有什麼不一樣？如果有另一個解釋也成立，你怎麼分？」
- **Failure:** accepting "it would change a lot" without the specific thing that would change.

### 12. Priority

- **Opens:** the ordering the user already believes but has never stated.
- **Shape:** 「如果只能保住一項，你保哪一項？失去另一項的代價是什麼？」
- **Failure:** a forced choice among three items, which produces a ranking rather than a criterion.
  Two items, once.

### 13. Alternative

- **Opens:** whether an explanation was chosen or merely arrived first.
- **Shape:** 「同樣的現象，還有另一種解釋嗎？你怎麼在這兩者之間取捨？」
- **Failure:** the agent proposing the alternative. Ask what else could produce the same result and
  let the user name it.

### 14. Steelman

- **Opens:** the strength of the position, by making the user argue against himself.
- **Shape:** 「最不同意你的人會怎麼說？他手上最有力的一點是什麼？」
- **Failure:** this move is asked too early. Before the user's own position has a construction, the
  steelman has nothing to stand against, and the answer comes back as a slogan.

---

## Reading the answers

- **A one-word answer** means the question was too abstract or too costly. Narrow it with a concrete
  instance from what the user already said.
- **An answer that changes the subject** usually means the question touched a criterion the user has
  not decided. Name that, rather than repeating the question.
- **An answer that repeats the previous answer** means the mirror was wrong. Offer the correction and
  ask which part is off.
- **An answer that arrives with a full plan** means the user is building on an unstated premise. Ask
  for the premise before the plan is discussed again.
- **Silence** is thinking. The next question waits.
